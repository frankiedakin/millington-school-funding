District context [1] · Funding and student needs

School district context · verbatim

Millington receives a higher rate of Title 1 funding per pupil because the percentage of students who qualify as economically disadvantaged (28%) is higher than any other municipality. Because of this, our overall student spending is higher.

This funding is provided by the federal government.

Our note

The FY2025 audit reports $932,163 in Title I spending, approximately $379 per pupil. Subtracting it would leave approximately $13,818 per pupil, still the highest among Shelby County’s six municipal districts. This calculation does not adjust for all differences in student needs.

District context [2] · Academic achievement

School district context · verbatim

Millington’s population needs are very different than other municipalities. Comparing their performance isn’t equitable and doesn’t consider the full context of factors that affect student performance.

This being said, we have still improved dramatically. Third-grade reading proficiency has grown from 18.4% to 48%, while third-grade math proficiency has increased from 12.6% to 48% since 2020-21.

Our note

Third grade was the strongest bright spot, but every grade from 3–8 improved in both subjects from 2021 to 2026. Millington’s gains for grades 3–8 combined exceeded all five municipal peers in both reading and math, although proficiency rates remained lower. These comparisons involve different students each year.

School district context · verbatim

In fact, according to recent projections made as part of our strategic plan, we are estimated to grow our student population by 4.6% by 2030.

Our note

The district also serves the annexation reserve outside city limits, so city population and school enrollment cover different areas. Total growth does not establish school-age growth, and the district’s statement does not quantify those age changes. Its future forecast does not establish why past enrollment changed.

District’s closing statement · verbatim

Our community and the students we serve is unique and the topic of school funding is nuanced. No two districts are the same and their needs, funding bases, and assets vary significantly from place to place.

We have always been, and will continue to be, good stewards of public dollars. Our priority is serving our students.

Strong schools are the foundation of a strong community, and we are proud of the progress happening across Millington Municipal Schools.

Our schools and our city succeed together. As Millington continues to grow, we remain committed to building on our progress, making responsible decisions, and ensuring every child has the opportunity to succeed.

Sources and clarification notes

  1. District statements. Verbatim wording from the district’s “Clarifying Context About Our Schools” graphic and the accompanying Facebook text supplied for this review. Paragraph layout and visual emphasis have been adapted. Reproducing a district statement does not mean that every figure in it has been independently verified.
  2. Title I and the FY2025 spending comparison. The district’s FY2025 audit, printed page 75, reports $932,163 of Title I expenditures, Assistance Listing 84.010. This is audited spending, not an award allocation or a separately reported payment per eligible student. Dividing by the guide’s 2,461-student October 1 K–12 enrollment gives $378.77 per pupil across all students. The guide’s reviewed FY2025 state-data extract reports $34,939,339.27 in operating expenditures for Millington. The calculation is ($34,939,339.27 − $932,163) ÷ 2,461 = $13,818.44. Arlington’s corresponding unadjusted amount is $59,451,641.15 ÷ 4,341 = $13,695.38. The other municipal districts’ unadjusted amounts are Bartlett $13,146.06, Collierville $13,256.13, Germantown $13,282.40 and Lakeland $12,437.33. Millington remains above each even though the comparison leaves their Title I spending included. This is a subtraction check for FY2025, not a revised official spending measure or a finding about all 11 years. Per-pupil inputs come from the School Funding Explorer’s existing reviewed extract of Tennessee’s FY2025 Annual Statistical Report, Table 51. The audit amount was checked directly for this revision. The original state workbook could not be refreshed during this check, so the retained state-data inputs have not been independently reverified in this revision.
  3. Assessment results. Tennessee Department of Education’s assessment dashboard for Millington, district 00798. The table uses the published display values from its historical results file, decoded with the corresponding label index and report definitions. Retrieved November 2, 2026. All six available years, 2021–2026, are shown.
  4. Third-grade baseline. The dashboard reports 2021 third-grade reading at 17.1% and math at 11.2%. The district’s statement uses 18.4% and 12.6%. The source or student-population difference has not been reconciled. The district’s statement is preserved as written, and the table retains the state dashboard’s figures.
  5. Third-grade accountability. The district’s 2026 TISA accountability report identifies third-grade reading as a required accountability goal. This provides a reason to report it separately. The report also provides broader measures and acknowledges uneven results across grades.
  6. Enrollment projection. The published five-year strategic plan projects enrollment increasing from 2,643 in 2025–26 to 2,740 in 2030–31, approximately 3.7%. The graphic states 4.6% by 2030. The baseline, time period or revision behind that difference has not been reconciled. Both are projections, and the district’s wording above is preserved.
  7. Service area. The City’s school-district description includes the annexation reserve. This establishes a boundary difference, not a count of students living outside the city or a claim that Millington is the only municipal district with such a boundary.
